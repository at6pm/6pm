# How much can I spend per day? The safe-to-spend method

Take your take-home pay, subtract bills, savings goals and whatever you pay yourself first, then divide what’s left by the days until payday, counting today. That’s your safe-to-spend number. On a $4,000 paycheck with typical big-city bills, it comes out near $37 a day, not $133. 6pm works it out every morning.

## Why "pay ÷ 30" lies to you

$4,000 ÷ 30 = $133 a day. Lovely. Brunch, a DoorDash order, a Target run that was supposed to be just toothpaste. All fine at $133.

Except that number quietly includes your rent, your student loan and your car insurance. Those leave first, they leave big, and they don’t care how your week went. Spend at the $133 pace and the last ten days of the month turn into a cooking show called "What Can I Make With Rice."

The fix isn’t a stricter budget. It’s a more honest division.

## The safe-to-spend formula

This is how 6pm does it, and you can do it on a napkin:

1. **Take-home pay**, after tax and deductions.
2. **Minus bills**: rent, utilities, internet, phone, insurance, loan and card repayments, money you owe a friend.
3. **Minus pots**: each savings goal gets a monthly set-aside, which is (goal − what you have) ÷ months left, rounded up to the nearest $50.
4. **Minus Future you**: the percentage you pay yourself before anything else. Say 10%.
5. What’s left is **free money**. Divide it by **the days until payday, counting today**, and round down to the dollar.

<!-- media: diagram-formula -->

Bills first. Future you second. The rest is yours, one day at a time.

## A worked example: $4,000 take-home, rented apartment

Say you’re paid $4,000 a month and there are 30 days until the next payday, today included.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Take-home pay | $4,000 |
| − Rent | $1,600 |
| − Utilities and internet | $160 |
| − Phone | $55 |
| − Student loan | $240 |
| − Car insurance | $130 |
| − Pot: "Tokyo" ($1,800 in 6 months) | $300 |
| − Future you (10%) | $400 |
| **= Free money** | **$1,115** |
| ÷ Days to payday | 30 |
| **Safe to spend today** | **$37** |

$1,115 ÷ 30 = $37.17, rounded down to $37. That has to cover groceries, gas, coffee, lunches, Ubers and the occasional "I deserve this."

Is $37 a lot? Depends on your city. The point is that it’s true. You can plan around a true number. You can only be surprised by a flattering one.

### Where the slack is

- Future you at 5% instead of 10% adds $200 of free money, about $6 a day.
- Push Tokyo to nine months and the pot needs $200 a month instead of $300.
- When the student loan ends, that $240 stops leaving. In 6pm a bill can have a last month, after which it simply stops.

There’s no universal right number. There’s the right number for your plan.

## What happens after a big day

Here’s where a daily number beats a monthly budget: it rebalances on its own.

<!-- media: close-tomorrow -->

Say you spend right on $37 for four days. On the morning of day five you have $967 left for 26 days. Still $37.

Then Saturday happens. Concert, dinner, a merch shirt you will wear exactly once. $160.

| | Left | Days | Per day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning of day 5 | $967 | 26 | $37 |
| After Saturday | $807 | 25 | $32 |
| Sunday, spent $15 | $792 | 24 | $33 |

One big night costs you $5 a day for the rest of the cycle. Nothing is "blown." Spend light the next day and the number creeps back up. That’s the whole method: overspend and the days ahead shrink a little, underspend and they grow.

6pm’s companion, a small sun by default, might put it as "Big day. Tomorrow’s $32, not $37. Month still lands." It teases the habit, never the person. If you’d rather have none of that, serious mode turns the jokes and the companion off.

With the numbers above, on the morning of day five you have $967. Spend $350 and $617 is left for 25 days: **$24 a day** until payday. Yes, you can. You’ll also be making a lot of sandwiches.

## FAQ

### How do I calculate how much I can spend per day?

Subtract bills, savings goals and what you pay yourself first from your take-home pay. Divide what’s left by the days until payday, counting today, and round down.

### Should I divide my monthly income by 30?

No. That number includes money that already belongs to your landlord and lenders. Divide your free money, the part left after bills and savings.

### What if I go over my daily number?

The extra is spread across the remaining days, so each one gets a little smaller. Spend less than the number and the days ahead grow instead.

### Does this work if I’m paid every two weeks or my income varies?

6pm supports payday on the same date each month, on the last day of the month, or "it varies," where you confirm each payday. Until you do, the cycle runs by calendar month.

### Do credit card and loan payments count?

Yes, as bills, so they’re set aside before your daily number is worked out. Card spending itself counts on the day it happens.
