# Small spends add up: six moments everyone knows (this week's cards)

Money rarely disappears in one big purchase. It leaks through small ones that repeat: the afternoon coffee, the dinner someone will "pay back later", a storage plan that charges every month. This week we drew six cards about those moments. Under each one is the real maths and one small tip, with no judgement.

## 1. "It's only $6"

![A receipt of a $6 iced latte every day, times 30 days, coming to $180](/images/blog/small-spends-add-up-six-moments/01-45k.jpg)

$6 a cup, one a day, 30 days: **$180**. Nobody thinks $6 is a lot. $180 is a different conversation.

**Tip:** for small spends that repeat, don't look at the single price. Multiply by 30 (or by 22 if it's a workdays-only habit). If you're happy with that number, keep buying it. The coffee isn't the problem. Now you just know what it really costs.

## 2. Six types of people when the bill arrives

![Cover of the carousel "6 types of people when the bill arrives", with a $49.60 hotpot bill for 6 people](/images/blog/small-spends-add-up-six-moments/02-chia-bill.jpg)

A $49.60 hotpot dinner for six. Every table has the "I'll send it later" friend, the one who splits it to the cent ($8.27 each), the one who pays for everyone and never asks back, the one who forgot their wallet again, the fastest QR in the room, and the one with a spreadsheet open at the table.

**Tip:** settle everyone's share the same night, while people still remember what they ate. Leave it a week and it becomes "wait, did I even order that?"

In 6pm, a receipt of roughly $20 or more offers **Split**: pick how many people (up to 20), and only your share is saved, with a note naming the total. Who pays whom back is still between you and your friends.

## 3. Dating budget: month 1 vs month 12

![Two date receipts: month 1 comes to $74.00, month 12 comes to $3.80](/images/blog/small-spends-add-up-six-moments/03-hen-ho.jpg)

Month 1: dinner with a river view, flowers, couple seats at the cinema, a car to pick them up: **$74**. Month 12: street snacks for two, a movie on a laptop, one large milk tea with two straws, a walk home: **$3.80**. About 20 times cheaper. Not one bit less fun.

**Tip:** a long relationship doesn't mean merging everything. Plenty of couples go "yours, mine and ours": put a little toward shared goals (a trip, the wedding) and keep everyday spending private.

6pm has **Budget together** for exactly that: shared pots you each put money toward, while neither of you sees the other's own spending. It's free. More in [budgeting as a couple](/blog/budgeting-as-a-couple).

## 4. iPhone storage almost full: the 5 stages

![The five stages of almost-full iPhone storage, from denial to accepting a bigger storage plan](/images/blog/small-spends-add-up-six-moments/04-bo-nho.jpg)

Denial ("Not now", for the 12th time). Bargaining (delete 400 screenshots, free up exactly 300 MB). Anger. Depression. And acceptance: upgrade the storage plan, one more monthly charge, forever.

One subscription is small. Five of them (storage, music, video, a language app, an AI tool) add up to a second utility bill.

**Tip:** open Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions on your iPhone and read the whole list once. Which one did you forget you're still paying for?

In 6pm, a charge that repeats every month can be added as a **bill**: each one is set aside before your daily number is worked out, so the statement holds no surprises.

## 5. Running: the cheapest sport in the world

![A running receipt: shoes, GPS watch, half-marathon entry, shirt and socks, and energy gels, $400 in total](/images/blog/small-spends-add-up-six-moments/05-chay-bo.jpg)

"All you need is a pair of shoes." Running shoes $95, a GPS watch $230, a half-marathon entry $35, a "technical" shirt and socks $26, energy gels $14. Total: **$400**. And three weeks of running so far.

**Tip:** a hobby that's good for you is worth paying for. Just count it as one package, not a string of separate buys. Once you know what a race season costs, you can set it aside bit by bit instead of paying for it all at once.

6pm has **pots** for goals like a race, a trip or the holidays. Each month a pot takes its share, (goal − what you already have) ÷ months left, before your daily number is worked out.

## 6. "This kind of rich"

![A fill-in-the-blank card: I don't need supercar rich, I want the kind of rich where I add extra toppings without checking the price](/images/blog/small-spends-add-up-six-moments/06-giau-kieu.jpg)

I don't need supercar rich. I want this kind of rich: adding extra toppings without checking the price. Cracking my phone screen and still having a normal lunch. Reaching the checkout without doing maths in the queue.

None of those are about big money. They're about **a buffer**: knowing what you have left, so one surprise doesn't ruin the day.

**Tip:** a buffer starts with knowing one number. In 6pm that's **Safe to spend today**: your pay minus bills, pots and Future you, spread evenly until payday. It's waiting for you every morning.

## FAQ

### How do I find out what small spends cost me each month?

Log every spend for one week, even the small ones, then scale it up to a month. Most people are surprised most by drinks, delivery fees and subscriptions.

### How do I split a group bill without the awkwardness?

Settle each person's share the same night, with the total and the number of people written down. In 6pm, a receipt of roughly $20 or more offers Split, and only your share is saved.
