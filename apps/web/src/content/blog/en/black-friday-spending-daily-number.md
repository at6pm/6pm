# Surviving Black Friday 2026 with one daily number

To avoid overspending on Black Friday, stop reading the discount and start reading your days. Turn the whole cart into how much less you’ll have each day until payday. A $300 cart on a typical $37-a-day plan drops you to $21 a day for the next 17 days. 6pm shows that number before you hit Place order.

## The dates, so they don’t ambush you

- **Black Friday**: Friday, 27 November 2026.
- **Cyber Monday**: Monday, 30 November 2026.
- **The "early Black Friday" deals**: they start landing in your inbox weeks before the day.

Four days of sales, wrapped around a long weekend, right before December. Your wallet is not up against a deal. It’s up against a season.

## "40% off" is the wrong number

A discount tells you what you didn’t spend. Your bank balance only cares about what you did. The question that actually matters is: if I buy this, what does every day between now and payday look like?

### A worked example: $300 on Black Friday

Take the plan from [how much can I spend per day](/blog/how-much-can-i-spend-per-day): $4,000 take-home, bills and a savings pot set aside, 10% to Future you. That leaves $1,115 of free money for a 30-day pay cycle, or **$37 a day**.

Say you’re paid on the 15th. Black Friday is day 13 of the cycle. If you’ve spent right on your number so far, you wake up on the 27th with $671 left for 18 days. Still $37.

Now the cart: a $300 TV that was "$499."

| | Left | Days | Per day |
|---|---|---|---|
| Black Friday morning | $671 | 18 | $37 |
| After the $300 cart | $371 | 17 | $21 |

That TV isn’t $300. It’s **$16 a day, every day, until 14 December**. That’s roughly a lunch out, every day, for two and a half weeks.

You might still want it. Fine. Now you’re buying it at its real price.

## Five steps: close the books before you check out

### 1. Cart everything, buy nothing (yet)

A cart is a waiting room, not a promise. Add freely. Countdown timers are there to rush exactly the thinking you’re about to do.

### 2. Ask 6pm one question

Open Ask 6pm and type: "Can I afford $300 on Friday?"

Your iPhone works it out from your own records: free money left, days to payday. The answer shows what your daily number becomes after the purchase, like the table above. An AI model only reads your question and words the reply; the maths stays on the phone. Free gets 10 questions a day through the server and unlimited ones in on-device mode.

If the after-number makes you wince, drop something and ask again. The third phone case. The air fryer you’re buying because it’s an air fryer.

### 3. Pay how you like, and let 6pm catch it

- **Apple Pay in store**: with the one-time Shortcuts automation set up, each tap lands in your Inbox.
- **Online by card**: forward card alerts and order emails to your phone’s 6pm address, like k7m2qx@at6pm.app. An AI model reads each one and tells a payment from a shipping update, a refund or a promo.
- **Cash, or paying a friend back on Venmo for the group order**: Quick add, three taps.

When your card’s alert and the store’s receipt both show up for one order, they merge into a single spend marked "Seen 2 ways." Nothing counts twice.

### 4. Remember the number already moved

Anything caught but not yet confirmed already counts against today. You don’t have to wait for the close to see the damage. That’s the point: the number stays honest during the sale, not after it.

### 5. At 6pm, close the day

Every spend comes up as a receipt. Keep, Edit, or Not mine. The last one gets the CLOSED stamp. About a minute. On Black Friday maybe two.

The companion will have notes. Something like "Logged. I won’t tell anyone." It teases the habit, never you. Prefer silence? Serious mode turns the jokes off.

## Buy now, pay later is still "pay"

Afterpay, Klarna, card installment plans: they make today’s cart feel light and the next six weeks feel heavy.

In 6pm, an installment plan is a **bill**. Add it to your plan, give it a last month, and each payment is set aside before your daily number is worked out. So a four-payment purchase shows up as a slightly tighter number now, not a surprise in January.

## Next year: give the sale its own pot

If you know you’ll shop every November, don’t let it be an ambush. Make a pot called "Black Friday." 6pm works out each month’s set-aside as (goal − what you have) ÷ months left, rounded up to the nearest $50. Start a $600 pot in January and it asks for about $100 a month at first, and less once you’re ahead.

By November the money is already sitting there, and your daily number doesn’t flinch when you check out.

## FAQ

### How do I not overspend on Black Friday?

Decide your limit before you browse, cart items instead of buying instantly, and convert the cart total into dollars per day until payday. Seeing "$16 a day for 17 days" changes more minds than "40% off."

### Does 6pm connect to my bank or store accounts?

No. It never asks for a bank login or a store password. It reads what you send it: Apple Pay taps through Shortcuts, receipt photos, forwarded emails, and what you say or type.

### Will Ask 6pm tell me not to buy something?

No. It shows what your daily number becomes after the purchase. The decision is yours, and it isn’t financial advice.

### What if I return something after Black Friday?

Fix it at the close: edit the amount or mark it Not mine. A forwarded refund email is recognized as a refund, not a new spend.

### Is 6pm on Android?

Not yet. 6pm is on iPhone first; Android comes later.
