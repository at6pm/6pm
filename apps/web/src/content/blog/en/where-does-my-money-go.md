# Where does my money go? The small-leak audit

If your money seems to vanish, it's rarely one big purchase. It's the $4 to $15 spends that repeat: delivery fees, lattes, forgotten subscriptions, Venmo splits you never requested back, one-click Amazon buys. Added up monthly, they can easily pass $400. The way to find them is to capture every small spend for seven days, then look at the total.

## Why small spends hide better than big ones

You remember buying a $1,200 laptop for years. A $6 latte is forgotten before the cup is empty. Our brains are bad at adding up small, frequent, scattered amounts, and tap-to-pay removed the last moment of friction where you'd notice money leaving.

The result is familiar: paycheck lands on the 1st, and by the 20th you're doing mental math at the grocery store, asking where it all went.

## Seven leaks, added up monthly

These are **examples**, not statistics: a plausible month for someone in a US city. Swap in your own numbers to see your real total.

### 1. The daily latte

$6 a coffee, 15 workdays a month: **$90**. It's usually the biggest leak nobody counts as big.

### 2. Delivery fees, service fees and tips

The burrito isn't the leak. The add-ons are. Say delivery, service fees and tip add about $9 to each DoorDash or Uber Eats order, eight orders a month: **$72** just to get food from the restaurant to your door.

### 3. Subscriptions you forgot you have

Streaming, music, extra cloud storage, a photo app, the fitness trial that quietly converted. Say four subscriptions averaging $12: **$48** a month, charged while you sleep.

### 4. Venmo splits you never collected

You covered the Uber, the pizza, the concert tickets "and I'll Venmo you." Some of it never comes back, and you don't chase $18. Say **$40** a month quietly absorbed.

### 5. One-click Amazon buys

A phone stand, a better charger, a gadget you saw on TikTok. Three a month at $25: **$75**. Fast shipping makes "might as well" very easy.

### 6. Convenience-store and gas-station runs

Snacks, an energy drink, gum at the register. Ten stops at $5: **$50**.

### 7. Late-night rides and food

Uber home instead of the train, a slice on the way. Four nights at $15: **$60**.

That's over $14 a day leaving without a single purchase feeling "big." Over a year, past $5,000.

None of these spends is wrong. Coffee is good, splitting dinner with friends is good, a phone stand might change your life. The problem is you didn't *choose* them. They just happened.

## How to run a 7-day small-leak audit

<!-- media: quick-add-clip -->

### Step 1: Capture everything, even $2

Seven days, no exceptions: parking, the vending machine, the ATM fee. The hard part is completeness, which is why it helps to let your phone catch spends instead of typing them (see [how to track spending automatically on iPhone](/blog/track-spending-automatically-iphone)).

### Step 2: Group by reason, not just category

"Food" is a category. "Bored," "convenient," "on sale" and "everyone else was" are reasons. Leaks live in reasons.

### Step 3: Fix one leak, not seven

Don't cut everything. Pick the one you'd miss least. Maybe you pick up instead of getting delivery twice a week, or you finally cancel the trial. One change you keep beats seven you drop by Friday.

### Step 4: Look again in a week

Not to judge yourself, but to see whether your daily number got a bit roomier.

## FAQ

### How do I figure out where my money goes each month?

Capture every spend, including the $2 ones, for at least seven days, then add them up by group. Most people are surprised by repeated small spends, not big purchases.

### What counts as a "money leak"?

A spend that repeats often without you really choosing it: delivery fees, forgotten subscriptions, uncollected splits, sale-driven buys. There's no fixed dollar threshold.

### Should I cut out coffee and takeout to save money?

You don't have to. Pick the leak you'd miss least and shrink that first. Small changes you keep usually beat cutting everything and quitting.
