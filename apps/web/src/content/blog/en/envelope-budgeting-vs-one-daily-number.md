# Envelope budgeting vs one daily number: which method fits you?

Envelope budgeting gives every category its own limit and stops you when an envelope runs dry. It suits people who overspend in specific places. A daily number sets bills, goals and savings aside first, then spreads the rest evenly to payday. It suits people who quit budgets because of too many categories. 6pm uses the daily number.

## Envelope budgeting, explained fairly

The classic version is literal: withdraw cash on payday, split it into labeled envelopes (groceries, gas, eating out, fun), and spend only from the matching envelope. When it’s empty, that category is done until next payday ([Ramsey Solutions](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/budgeting/envelope-system-explained), [Chase](https://www.chase.com/personal/banking/education/budgeting-saving/envelope-budget-system)).

What it gets right:

- **Hard limits you can see.** An empty envelope is impossible to argue with.
- **It targets the leak.** Most people don’t overspend everywhere. They overspend on takeout, or Target, or concerts. An envelope for that one category works.
- **It makes trade-offs physical.** Moving $20 from "fun" to "groceries" is a decision you feel.

Where it struggles:

- **Most spending isn’t cash anymore.** Apple Pay, subscriptions, Venmo splits and online orders don’t come out of a paper envelope. Digital envelope apps solve this, but then you’re maintaining a dozen balances.
- **Fixed bills don’t fit well.** Even fans of the method tend to keep rent and auto-paid bills outside the envelopes.
- **Categories multiply.** "Coffee" splits into "coffee with friends" and "coffee alone at the airport." A dozen envelopes is a dozen things to babysit.

## The 50/30/20 rule

Popularized by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in *All Your Worth*, it splits after-tax income into three buckets ([InCharge](https://www.incharge.org/financial-literacy/budgeting-saving/50-30-20-rule/)):

- **50% needs**: housing, groceries, utilities, transportation, minimum debt payments.
- **30% wants**: dining out, shopping, entertainment.
- **20% savings and extra debt payments.**

It’s simple, which is its whole charm. The catch: in an expensive city, rent alone can eat most of the 50%. And you still have to decide whether a work lunch is a need or a want, then track three running totals across the month.

## Zero-based budgeting, briefly

Give every dollar a job until income minus assignments equals zero. It’s thorough and it works for people who enjoy the process. For people who don’t, it’s a lot of weekly upkeep.

## One daily number

This is how 6pm works:

1. **Bills** set aside first: rent, utilities, phone, loan and card repayments.
2. **Pots** for goals: each takes (goal − what you have) ÷ months left, rounded up to $50.
3. **Future you** gets paid next, a percentage you choose.
4. What’s left is free money, divided by the days until payday, counting today.

<!-- media: diagram-formula -->

You get one number every morning: **Safe to spend today**. For a $4,000 take-home with typical city bills, a pot and 10% to Future you, it lands around $37 (the full maths is in [how much can I spend per day](/blog/how-much-can-i-spend-per-day)).

Overspend today and the next days shrink a little. Underspend and they grow. Nothing "breaks," because there’s nothing to break. The number simply re-balances.

Look closely and it isn’t "no envelopes." Bills, pots and Future you are envelopes, the important ones. The difference is that everyday spending becomes one envelope that refills daily, instead of ten that drain at different speeds.

## Which one should you pick?

**Envelopes, if** you have one or two categories that keep running away from you and you want a hard stop on them. Especially if you still use cash.

**50/30/20, if** you want a quick sanity check on the shape of your spending and you’re happy reviewing it monthly.

**Zero-based, if** you like spreadsheets, enjoy assigning money, and will actually sit down every week.

**One daily number, if** you’ve tried budgets before and quit because of the categories, you think in days rather than months, and the only question you want answered is "can I spend this today?"

### The honest trade-off

A daily number doesn’t cap individual categories. 6pm sorts your spending into categories and shows you where the money went, but it won’t stop you at "$200 on dining out." If that hard stop is exactly what you need, envelopes do it better. You can exclude a category from the daily number in 6pm, but categories have no limits.

## FAQ

### What is envelope budgeting?

You split your money into envelopes, physical or digital, one per spending category. You spend only from the matching envelope, and when it’s empty that category is done until payday.

### Is envelope budgeting better than the 50/30/20 rule?

They solve different problems. Envelopes give hard per-category limits; 50/30/20 gives a simple overall shape to check monthly. Envelopes take more upkeep.

### What is a daily spending number?

It’s your free money, after bills, savings goals and paying yourself first, divided by the days until payday. You spend against that one number instead of category budgets.

### Can 6pm set category limits?

No. Categories in 6pm show where your money went; they don’t have limits. You can leave a category out of the daily number.

### Can I combine envelopes with a daily number?

Yes. Use pots as envelopes for big goals, set Future you as your savings rate, and let 6pm turn the rest into one number.
