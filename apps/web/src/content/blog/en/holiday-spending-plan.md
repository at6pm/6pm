# Holiday spending plan: pay future you first

A holiday spending plan has three moves: write down what the season will cost (gifts, travel, food, parties), set that total aside from your next paychecks before anything else, and keep paying Future you as usual. Whatever is left becomes your daily number. 6pm runs this with a Holidays pot, so December doesn’t borrow from January.

## Why holidays wreck January

December spending feels like it happens in December. It doesn’t. It happens on a card in December and lands in your account in January, right next to rent, the gym that auto-renewed and the heating bill that doubled.

For a sense of scale, the National Retail Federation’s 2025 survey found US shoppers planned to spend [$890.49 per person](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-to-spend-second-highest-amount-on-record-according-to-nrf-holiday-survey) on the winter holidays, $627.93 of it on gifts. That’s an average, not a target. Your number might be half that or double. The point is that it’s a real line in your budget, not "a few presents."

## Step 1: list what the season actually costs

Not a guess. A list. Something like:

| Line | Planned |
|---|---|
| Gifts (8 people) | $400 |
| Flight home | $280 |
| Food and hosting | $120 |
| Office party, drinks, secret Santa | $60 |
| Decorations and wrapping | $40 |
| **Total** | **$900** |

Per-person gift caps help more than anything else here. "$50 for each sibling, $25 for each coworker" is a decision you make once, calmly, instead of twenty times in a crowded store.

Book flights early if you can. Once a line is booked, it stops being a guess and becomes a number.

## Step 2: set it aside first

In 6pm, that total becomes a **pot**. Each month the pot takes its share before your daily number is worked out: (goal − what you already have) ÷ months left, rounded up to the nearest $50.

Say you’ve already got $200 put away and you start now, in late October, with two months left before the holidays:

- ($900 − $200) ÷ 2 = **$350** set aside each month.

Wait until November and there’s one month left, so the same pot needs ($900 − $200) ÷ 1 = **$700** from a single paycheck. Here’s what each does to a typical plan.

## FAQ

### How much should I budget for the holidays?

Start from a list, not a percentage: gifts per person, travel, food, parties, decorations. For reference, NRF’s 2025 survey put planned spending at about $890 per person, but your number should come from your list and your pay.

### How do I save for Christmas if it’s only weeks away?

Divide what you still need by the paychecks left, and set that aside first. If the result squeezes your daily number too hard, trim the list now rather than borrowing from January.

### Should I stop saving during the holidays?

That’s your call, and 6pm doesn’t give financial advice. It keeps Future you paid first by default and shows exactly what lowering it would change, so the decision is on purpose.
